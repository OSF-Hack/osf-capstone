# Applicant validation findings

Reviewed: 21 September 2026

Source: `Nigerian Driver’s Licence Applicant Validation (Responses)`

## Sample and eligibility

- Eight responses were recorded.
- Three respondents answered that they had not personally applied for,
  renewed or reissued a Nigerian driver's licence and were excluded.
- Five respondents confirmed direct experience and consented to anonymous use
  of their responses.
- The five eligible responses covered Kaduna (3), Niger (1) and Plateau (1).

This was a small convenience sample. It validates recurring applicant
experiences but does not estimate national prevalence.

## Repeated findings

| Finding | Eligible responses | Product implication |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Reported four or more physical return visits | 3 of 5 | Keep the remote tracking and escalation journey prominent |
| Reported repeated transport expenses | 3 of 5 | Emphasise avoiding unnecessary centre visits |
| Described biometric or capture-related friction | 3 of 5 | Keep the neutral capture-exception route |
| Named at least one traceable signal that would show a complaint was being handled | 5 of 5 | Preserve reference, timeframe, named-next-step and written-confirmation guidance |
| Trusted a physical FRSC office most | 2 of 5 | Keep the official centre directory |
| Trusted the FRSC telephone line most | 1 of 5 | Keep the verified telephone route |
| Trusted none of the listed escalation channels | 2 of 5 | Do not overstate institutional trust or promise resolution |

The traceable signals requested across the sample included a complaint
reference number, status notification, written resolution, response timeframe,
named next step, responsible agency and SMS or email confirmation.

## Other signals

- Two respondents explicitly chose biometric issues as the first problem to
  solve, while one chose tracking. Two left the question unanswered.
- One respondent said they would save, print or share an official checklist,
  two answered maybe, one answered no and one did not answer. This does not
  support claiming strong demand for the action card.
- Three respondents allowed an anonymous quotation, but the submission should
  prefer aggregate findings because free-text wording may still be identifying.

## Evidence decision

**PASS: applicant problem validation.** More than two eligible applicants
independently confirmed recurring visibility, repeat-visit, capture or
escalation problems.

**UNTESTED: prototype usability.** The final participant form intentionally
removed the prototype task. These responses therefore cannot establish that
participants reached the tracker, understood the interface or completed the
golden journey without assistance.

## Product decision

### Keep

- official tracker as the first route;
- neutral biometric and capture-exception guidance;
- physical centre and telephone escalation options;
- copyable or printable action record;
- explicit privacy and evidence boundaries; and
- offline access to previously loaded guidance.

### Do not add before submission

- status predictions or invented completion dates;
- claims that CivicRoute NG resolves FRSC cases;
- notification features without an official integration;
- sensitive applicant-data collection; or
- a major workflow redesign based on this small sample.

## Fast usability follow-up

If time permits, send the live link to three eligible respondents and ask each
to complete one scenario without instruction. Record only whether they found
the tracker, found an escalation route, and what confused them. Keep this
separate from the survey findings.
